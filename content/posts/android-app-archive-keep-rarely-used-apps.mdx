Every Android user eventually faces a cluttered app drawer, with dozens of programs opened only once a year. I recently discovered that Android’s built‑in App Archive feature offers a middle ground between keeping an app and uninstalling it. By archiving, the app’s icon fades, its code and temporary files are stripped, yet the data and icon remain for instant restoration. This trick saves storage without the hassle of re‑installing, and it silences unwanted notifications. For anyone juggling limited space on a 128 GB device, it’s a game‑changer.

<Callout type="takeaway">Archiving lets you hide rarely used apps while preserving data, cutting storage without a full uninstall.</Callout>

## What happened
Android introduced an “Archive” option in the app‑info screen (available on Android 13 and later). When you choose Archive, the system removes the APK package, cache files, runtime permissions, and stops all background processes and notifications. The app’s icon stays on the home screen or app drawer in a faded state, and tapping it triggers a quick reinstall that restores the app exactly where you left it.

The feature strikes a balance: it frees more space than leaving the app untouched, but far less than a complete uninstall because the app’s data folder remains. Users report that archived apps no longer generate notification bubbles, and the faded icons serve as a visual cue for “rarely used”. Re‑installing is almost instantaneous, as the Play Store only needs to download the APK again while preserving existing user data.

## Why it matters
For phones with modest internal storage—like the 128 GB handset mentioned in the source—every gigabyte counts. Archiving lets power users prune their app landscape without losing login credentials, saved files, or in‑app progress. It also reduces background battery drain and network usage caused by dormant apps. The trade‑off is that storage savings are modest, so the feature is most valuable for apps that are large enough to matter but still needed occasionally (e.g., travel planners, niche utilities).

## How to think about it
Start by reviewing your app usage statistics (Settings → Digital Wellbeing → App usage). If an app hasn’t been opened in the past three months and you can imagine needing its data later, archive it. For apps you haven’t used in over six months—or that are large games—uninstall outright. Keep a mental “restore window” of a few weeks: if you need the archived app, tap the faded icon and let Android reinstall it. This workflow lets you maintain a lean device while avoiding the friction of re‑configuring apps from scratch.

<Callout type="warning">Archiving does not free enough space for large apps, and some archived apps may require you to re‑grant permissions after restoration.</Callout>

## FAQ
<FAQ>
  <Question q="How do I archive an app on Android?">Open Settings → Apps, select the app, tap the three‑dot menu, and choose “Archive”. The icon will fade, indicating it’s archived.</Question>
  <Question q="Will archiving delete my app’s saved data?">No. Archiving removes the executable and temporary files but leaves the app’s data directory intact, so all your settings and files remain.</Question>
  <Question q="Can I archive system apps or only user‑installed ones?">Only user‑installed apps can be archived. System apps are protected and must be disabled or left as‑is.</Question>
