A 48‑year‑old U.S. Marine veteran was killed after a speeding, intoxicated boater collided with her vessel on the Fox River Saturday. The victim, identified as Magdalena Jablonska, was out with family when 45‑year-old Richard Stevenson struck their boat at roughly 80 mph. Authorities say Stevenson’s blood‑alcohol level was twice the legal limit and he faces aggravated DUI and reckless homicide charges. The crash has revived scrutiny of boating safety and enforcement of alcohol limits on inland waterways.

<Callout type="takeaway">A drunk, high‑speed boat crash claimed a Marine veteran’s life and highlights gaps in water‑way safety enforcement.</Callout>

## What happened
Magdalena Jablonska, a former Marine who immigrated from Poland, was on the Fox River with her fiancé and dog when Stevenson’s high‑powered pontoon slammed into the back of their vessel. The impact killed Jablonska instantly and injured the other occupants.

Prosecutors allege Stevenson was driving a 80‑mph pontoon while under the influence, with a blood‑alcohol content reported at twice the legal limit. He has a two‑decade history of DUI‑related offenses, including multiple convictions for reckless driving and speeding, and now faces charges of aggravated DUI and reckless homicide.

## Why it matters
The incident underscores how alcohol‑impaired operation can turn recreational boating into a lethal activity, especially in congested inland waterways. Families and advocacy groups are calling for stricter enforcement and education to prevent similar tragedies. The case also puts pressure on local authorities to allocate resources for patrols and sobriety checkpoints on the river.

## How to think about it
If you enjoy boating, treat the water like any road: never operate a vessel while impaired, respect speed limits, and wear life jackets. Support local initiatives that fund safety patrols and consider advocating for mandatory sobriety testing on popular waterways.

<Callout type="warning">Never operate a boat under the influence of alcohol; impaired boating is a leading cause of fatal water accidents.</Callout>
