The Cleveland Browns have officially secured safety Grant Delpit on a new three‑year contract worth $48 million. The deal, reported by ESPN sources, extends Delpit through the 2029 season and includes a significant raise from his rookie deal. Keeping Delpit in Cleveland bolsters the Browns’ secondary and signals the franchise’s commitment to building around a young defensive core. The extension also has implications for the team’s salary‑cap strategy as they juggle other roster moves.

<Callout type="takeaway">Delpit’s $48 million extension locks a key defensive playmaker into Cleveland for the next three seasons.</Callout>

## What happened
Sources confirm the Browns and safety Grant Delpit have agreed to a three‑year, $48 million extension, reportedly finalized ahead of the league’s weekly transaction deadline. The contract replaces Delpit’s rookie deal, which paid him a modest salary as a second‑round pick, and reflects his rising value after a breakout season in Cleveland’s defense.

Delpit entered the 2025 season as a starter, posting consistent tackle numbers and solid coverage metrics that placed him among the league’s more reliable safeties. His ability to read plays and contribute in both run support and pass defense has made him a cornerstone of the Browns’ secondary.

## Why it matters
Locking Delpit in provides the Browns with stability at safety, a position that has seen turnover in recent years. The contract also signals Cleveland’s willingness to invest in young talent rather than pursuing high‑priced free agents, shaping the team’s long‑term roster philosophy. However, the sizable cap hit limits flexibility, forcing the front office to balance other positional needs against the financial commitment.
