Aneesah Morrow, the 2025 seventh‑overall pick, has been sent from the Connecticut Sun to the Toronto Tempo as the trade deadline looms. The move gives the Tempo a proven front‑court rebounder while the Sun acquire the rights to Maria Kliundikova and a 2028 second‑round pick. Both clubs are reshaping their rosters ahead of significant changes—Toronto hopes to spark a playoff push, and Connecticut is preparing for a relocation to Houston. The trade underscores how quickly WNBA teams must adapt in a tight market. Fans will be watching closely to see how Morrow fits into Toronto’s young, explosive guard core.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Tempo add a proven rebounder in Aneesah Morrow to boost their frontcourt.</Callout>

## What happened
The Connecticut Sun announced that forward Aneesah Morrow has been traded to the Toronto Tempo. In return, Toronto sent the rights to forward Maria Kliundikova and its 2028 second‑round pick to Connecticut. The Tempo also waived guard Tima Pouye to keep a 12‑player roster, and the trade will be finalized within 48 hours pending a physical.

Morrow, drafted seventh overall in the 2025 WNBA Draft, posted 11.6 points, 8.9 rebounds and 1.7 assists across 20 appearances this season. She shot 42.7% from the floor, leads the league with 10.8 defensive rebounds per 40 minutes, and holds a 23.5% total rebounding percentage—the second‑best in the league. Her performance includes a 21‑point, seven‑rebound effort in a recent loss to the Chicago Sky.

The Tempo sit at 10‑18 and are enduring a five‑game losing streak as they head into a road trip. Adding Morrow addresses their 13th‑ranked rebounding position out of 15 teams and pairs her with guard Kiki Rice and All‑Star Marina Mabrey to strengthen their frontcourt.

## Why it matters
For Toronto, securing a high‑energy rebounder could be the catalyst needed to climb the standings and secure a playoff berth in the second half of the season. Morrow’s ability to dominate the boards may alleviate the Tempo’s rebounding woes and give coach Becky Hammon more flexibility in line‑ups. Meanwhile, the Sun are in flux; the franchise will relocate to Houston next year, and the trade provides assets—Kliundikova’s rights and a future pick—to aid a rebuild in a new market. The transaction also highlights how WNBA teams balance immediate needs with long‑term planning under salary‑cap constraints.
