The Milwaukee Brewers struck a deadline deal on Saturday, bringing catcher Bo Naylor and right‑handed reliever Codi Heuer from Cleveland. In return, the Guardians receive outfielder Blake Perkins and pitcher Craig Yoho. The move addresses a sudden catching shortage after top prospect Jefferson Quero was sidelined with a biceps injury. By assigning Naylor and Heuer to Triple‑A Nashville, Milwaukee adds depth while preserving roster flexibility. The trade underscores the Brewers’ push to solidify the roster for the final stretch of the season.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Brewers added Bo Naylor and Codi Heuer to shore up catching depth and bullpen flexibility at the trade deadline.</Callout>

## What happened
The four‑player swap sends Bo Naylor, a 26‑year‑old catcher with major‑league experience, and reliever Codi Heuer to the Brewers organization. Cleveland receives outfielder Blake Perkins, a switch‑hitting former Gold Glove finalist, and right‑hander Craig Yoho, a change‑up specialist.

Both newcomers were promptly assigned to Triple‑A Nashville, giving Milwaukee a ready‑to‑call option behind the plate while Heuer bolsters the bullpen depth. Brewers president of baseball operations Matt Arnold said the club “wanted to protect the downside” and add another layer of catching depth as Quero recovers from injury.

## Why it matters
Catching depth has become a premium for Milwaukee; with Jefferson Quero limited to DH duties, the Brewers risk a vulnerability behind the plate during a crucial playoff push. Naylor’s experience on competitive teams provides a safety net, while Heuer adds a left‑handed arm that can bridge the gap left by Craig Yoho’s departure. Conversely, losing Perkins reduces outfield flexibility and Yoho’s change‑up removes a unique bullpen weapon, forcing the Brewers to rely more on existing arms.

## How to think about it
Fans should monitor Naylor’s performance at Triple‑A and his readiness for a call‑up if Quero’s recovery stalls. Heuer’s outings in Nashville will indicate whether he can fill a middle‑relief role or become a set‑up option. Evaluate the trade’s success by the Brewers’ ability to maintain defensive stability behind the plate and keep the bullpen effective in the final weeks of the season.
