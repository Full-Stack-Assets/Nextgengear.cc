Clay Holmes took the mound for a High‑A Brooklyn rehab start on Thursday, a key step as the trade deadline looms. He threw 73 pitches over 5 1⁄3 innings, allowing four runs (three earned) in a 6‑5 win over Winston‑Salem. The outing marked his first professional appearance since a fractured right fibula on May 15. With the June 28 deadline just days away, the performance fuels speculation about whether New York will keep him or move him in a deal.

## What happened
Holmes entered the game for the High‑A Brooklyn team, delivering 5 1⁄3 innings while issuing 73 pitches. He kept the Dash scoreless for the first two innings, then surrendered a run in each of the next four frames, ending with four runs (three earned) in a 6‑5 victory.

The right‑hander said he is “more than ready to pitch in the big leagues,” and Mets interim manager Andy Green noted that Holmes is “lengthened out to the point where you would expect him to start a Major League game.” Both the player and the front office acknowledged that a trade is “pretty likely,” adding uncertainty to his immediate future.

## Why it matters
Holmes represents a rare, healthy right‑hander on a Mets roster that has struggled with rotation depth. A strong rehab outing could boost his trade value, giving New York leverage to acquire prospects or address other needs before the deadline. Conversely, keeping him could provide a low‑risk arm for September call‑ups, but the team risks losing a potential asset if a deal materializes.

## How to think about it
Fans should track three signals: (1) Holmes’ workload in the next two starts, (2) any official statements from the Mets front office about trade discussions, and (3) roster moves involving other right‑handers before the June 28 deadline. If his innings stay low and the Mets announce a trade, expect a deal that includes a prospect or a bullpen arm.
