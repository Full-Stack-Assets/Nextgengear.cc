North Georgia was hit by a sudden cold front Tuesday evening, unleashing scattered storms, heavy rain, frequent lightning and gusty winds across the mountains and eastern metro Atlanta. The storm system produced 40‑50 mph wind gusts, hail, and flash‑flooding in counties such as DeKalb, Cobb, Fulton, Gilmer and Gordon. Tornado watches were issued as rotating storms touched down, leaving downed trees and power lines in their wake. Residents now face extensive cleanup as roads remain blocked and water levels recede. Understanding the scope of the damage helps emergency crews prioritize resources.

## What happened
The cold front moving through North Georgia on Tuesday evening sparked scattered thunderstorms that dumped several inches of rain in a short period, causing flash flooding in low‑lying areas. Strong lines of storms generated wind gusts of 40 mph in DeKalb, Cobb and Fulton counties and up to 50 mph in northern counties, toppling trees and snapping power lines.

County emergency managers reported hail, heavy rain, and isolated tornadoes that ripped through Gilmer and Gordon counties, leaving structural damage to homes and businesses. The National Weather Service issued multiple Severe Thunderstorm Warnings, and local authorities deployed sandbags and rescue teams to assist stranded motorists.

## Why it matters
The combination of high winds, hail and flash floods threatens critical infrastructure, disrupts commuter routes, and puts vulnerable populations at risk. Power outages affect thousands, and prolonged water damage can lead to mold and long‑term property loss, amplifying insurance costs and community recovery timelines.

## How to think about it
When severe weather strikes, prioritize safety: stay informed via NOAA alerts, avoid travel on flooded roads, and secure outdoor items that could become projectiles. After the storm, assess property for water intrusion, document damage for insurance, and follow local guidance for debris pickup. Building a personal emergency kit—including a battery‑powered radio, flashlights, and non‑perishable food—can reduce reliance on strained municipal services during the immediate aftermath.

<Callout type="warning">Do not attempt to drive through flooded roads; water depth can be deceptive and dangerous.</Callout>
