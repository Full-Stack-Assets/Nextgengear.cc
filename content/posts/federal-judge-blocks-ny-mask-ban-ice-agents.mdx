The federal judiciary just dealt a blow to New York’s effort to restrict how immigration officers appear in public. A Northern District judge issued a preliminary injunction that stops the state’s mask ban for ICE agents, citing the Supremacy Clause. The decision also leaves untouched a separate law that bars local‑federal immigration enforcement agreements. This ruling reshapes the balance between state authority and federal immigration policy. Stakeholders from civil‑rights groups to local law‑enforcement agencies are watching the outcome closely.

<Callout type="takeaway">New York’s mask ban for ICE agents is temporarily blocked because it conflicts with federal authority.</Callout>

## What happened
Judge Mae D'Agostino of the Northern District of New York granted a preliminary injunction against the state law that prohibited federal immigration agents from wearing masks and required them to display identification. The court found that the “face covering and identification acts” directly regulate federal officers, violating the Supremacy Clause.

New York Attorney General Letitia James and Governor Kathy Hochul issued statements after the ruling, emphasizing that the state will continue to oppose masked ICE agents and that the ban on 287(g) agreements remains lawful. The judge also refused to block New York’s separate law banning local‑federal immigration enforcement agreements.

## Why it matters
The decision underscores the constitutional hierarchy that federal immigration enforcement supersedes state attempts to impose operational restrictions. By enjoining the mask ban, the court preserves the federal government’s ability to deploy agents without state‑mandated attire rules, which could affect operational safety and anonymity in sensitive investigations. At the same time, the ruling leaves New York’s broader effort to limit cooperation with ICE intact, preserving a tool that state officials argue protects local communities from federal overreach.
