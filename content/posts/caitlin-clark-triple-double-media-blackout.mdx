Caitlin Clark posted her fourth career triple‑double, helping the Indiana Fever defeat the Portland Fire for a fifth straight win. Despite the on‑court performance, the Fever kept Clark and teammate Sophie Cunningham away from post‑game reporters. The media blackout came after head coach Stephanie White fielded questions about biological males competing in women’s sports, sparking a viral clip. The incident highlights the growing tension between the WNBA, activist groups, and the media. It also raises questions about how teams manage player access during politically charged moments.

## What happened
The Fever’s pre‑game press conference featured coach Stephanie White fielding a reporter’s query on whether she would welcome a biological male in the WNBA. White responded that she was “not educated enough” on the scientific aspects of transgender participation and did not rule out exclusion, emphasizing the need for “deeper and more layered conversations.”

After the Fever’s 105‑95 victory over the Seattle Storm, both Clark and Cunningham were absent from the post‑game media session. ESPN confirmed that neither player was made available, and the team’s public‑relations representative read a statement noting the organization had no knowledge of the outside rally supporting Cunningham’s stance.

The incident occurred amid a pre‑game rally outside Climate Pledge Arena supporting Cunningham’s comments and promoting Washington ballot initiatives aimed at restricting transgender athletes in girls’ sports. The controversy amplified when White’s answers were shared widely on social media, drawing criticism and support from different quarters.

## Why it matters
The media blackout illustrates how WNBA teams are navigating heightened scrutiny over gender‑inclusion policies. By limiting player access, the Fever signaled a protective stance for its athletes while attempting to control the narrative. This approach may influence future media protocols across the league, especially when games intersect with political activism. Fans, sponsors, and broadcasters are all affected, as the balance between free press and player privacy becomes increasingly contested.
