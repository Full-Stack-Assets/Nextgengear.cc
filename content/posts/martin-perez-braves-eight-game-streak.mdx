After seven straight victories, the club has limited opponents to four runs or fewer in each of its last eight games, a run‑prevention feat that has kept them atop the NL East. Starting pitcher Martin Perez, who delivered a seven‑inning, zero‑run gem against the Nationals last weekend, is slated to take the mound. His surprise appearance came after Reynaldo Lopez left the rotation with a knee injury, highlighting the depth of Atlanta’s staff. If the Braves hold the line, they could extend one of the most dominant defensive stretches of the season.

## What happened
The Braves have won seven consecutive games and have allowed four or fewer runs in each of their last eight outings, with three or fewer runs in the past five contests. Their run‑prevention success stems from solid defense and a favorable home‑run‑to‑fly‑ball ratio, compensating for middling underlying pitching metrics.

Martin Perez broke a stretch of mediocre outings with a dominant performance against the Washington Nationals: six strikeouts, two walks, and zero runs over a season‑high seven innings. The start came on short notice after Reynaldo Lopez suffered a knee injury, and Perez was added to the rotation less than an hour before tip‑off. On the season, Perez’s advanced stats sit at a 77 ERA‑, 104 FIP‑, and 99 xFIP‑, and his contract value is roughly $3.5 million.

The combination of Perez’s surprise effectiveness and the Braves’ defensive consistency has kept the team’s ERA among the league’s best since late July, despite a middle‑of‑the‑pack xFIP ranking.
